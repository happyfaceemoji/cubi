CUBI WITH CAMERA EYES (48 mm, 4 micro servos, 1S rechargeable) - PRINT & BUILD NOTES
(Older versions: v4_mini_screen/ = mini with a 1.3" screen, v3_80mm/ = 80 mm MG90S version.)

PRINTING ON THE BAMBU A1 MINI - 0.4 mm nozzle, everything in Bambu PLA Basic, ONE plate
  a1mini_plates/ponder_mini_all_parts_PLA_Basic.stl  shell (top face down), bottom plate, 4 legs (horn
                                                      pocket up), 4 shoes on their side (~27 g)
  (Single parts are in stl/ if you want to reprint just one.)

  Bambu Studio:
  - Printer: Bambu Lab A1 mini 0.4 nozzle. Filament: Bambu PLA Basic. Process: 0.20mm Standard
    (0.16 mm Fine looks better on a part this small, and is still under 2 hours).
  - Textured or smooth PEI, no glue. NO supports anywhere.
  - Shell and plate: 4 walls (the shell walls are 1.6 mm = 4 lines, so they print solid), 15% gyroid.
  - Legs: per-object settings, 4 walls, 40% infill.
  - Overhangs: the oval USB-C bevel (arched ends), small hole arches, the 1.4 mm eye bezels and a 2 mm speaker ledge.
    Default settings handle them. Two-colour tip: with an AMS lite, print the eye bezels in white.

  Dry the filament first, wash the plate, run Bed Leveling + Flow Dynamics Calibration.
  Print ONE leg + ONE shoe first and test them on a real micro-servo horn.

PARTS (see Cubi_BOM.pdf)
- 4x GH-S37D 3.7 g digital micro servos (20 x 9 x 22 mm incl. output; 90 deg travel; horns and screws included)
- Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense (camera + mic + battery charger built in) - LEFT EYE
- VL53L0X time-of-flight distance sensor, GY-530 board (designed for 20.5 x 15.5 mm; measure yours) - RIGHT EYE
- 1S LiPo 602035 (6 x 20 x 35 mm, 400-500 mAh); cut its plug, solder on a JST PH2.0 male/female pair
- E-Switch 100SP1T1B4M2QE mini toggle (smooth bushing, no nut; PCB pins: solder wires straight on)
- MAX98357A amp (leave its screw terminal off), 1511 speaker (15 x 11 x 3.5, 8 ohm 1 W)
- 1x 1000 uF 6.3 V capacitor 8 mm wide (battery bulk), 1x 470 uF 6.3-10 V 6.3 mm wide (servo supply); SMD or radial
- 2x 100 kOhm resistors (battery-voltage divider into D2)
- Copper tape, heat-shrink, 26 AWG silicone wire, foam tape, a thin strap (1N5819 diode now optional: servo-voltage drop)
- 4x M2x6 screws (bottom plate, heads sunk in counterbores, self-tapping into the corner bosses)

BUILD ORDER (the order the fit check proved works)
1. VL53L0X (RIGHT EYE) FIRST: behind the right eye is a solid wedge whose face tilts 30 deg down, with a
   pocket for the sensor's parts and a cone window out through the eye. Bring the board in from behind, a
   few mm toward the middle and low, then slide it UP the wedge face (sensor side against the face, header
   at the bottom) until it stops at the top of the pocket. Two dabs of hot glue on the edges.
2. XIAO ESP32S3 Sense (LEFT EYE): glue the camera module into the square socket behind the left eye, lens
   in the opening. Lift the board stack in ~15 mm back from its spot, then push it straight forward onto the
   camera; its USB-C pokes out of the hole in the LEFT wall. Hot-glue the stack edge to the left wall.
3. Check the eyes: USB-C into the XIAO through the side opening, run the self-test: camera and distance PASS.
4. Speaker between the two ledges on the right wall (behind the sensor wedge), sound side out, glue.
   Copper-tape touch pad under the middle of the top. Battery bulk cap (1000 uF) under the top, back-right.
5. Hip servos, one at a time: hold it ~9 mm toward the middle, lift it up past the tab pads, slide it out
   until the shaft pokes through the side hole and the tabs sit flat on the two pads. Drive the servo's own
   screws through the tab holes into the pads (they self-tap; pre-drill 1 mm if they're stiff). The pads
   are solid, so it doesn't matter where your servo's holes land. If the case touches the wall before the
   tabs touch the pads, that's fine; if the tabs touch first and the spline barely pokes out, sand the pads.
6. Power switch: solder its wires to the middle pin and one outer pin first, push the smooth bushing through the
   6.65 mm back hole from INSIDE (between the two rear servos) until the body drops into the shallow pocket, then
   glue the body to the wall (gel super glue or hot glue). No nut. Servo-supply capacitor (470 uF) in next to it.
7. Battery: stand it on its long edge between the fences on the bottom plate, strap the left end. Foam-tape
   the amp to the RIGHT half of the battery's front face (chips forward). Plug in, lift the plate in, 4 x M2x6.
8. Legs: centre each servo, glue a horn into each leg recess (arm down the leg), press the leg onto the
   spline ~30 deg FORWARD (front legs) or ~30 deg BACK (rear legs), not straight down, add the servo's centre screw. Push a shoe onto each peg with glue.

WIRING (the Wiring view in the 3D viewer shows every wire and the pin table)
- Battery -> PH2.0 plug -> toggle middle lug; toggle outer lug = switched battery. Off = battery fully
  disconnected.
- Switched battery (power splice) -> XIAO BAT+ pad (back of the XIAO, near the USB end), 1000 uF +, top of the
  100k/100k divider, 4 servo reds, 470 uF +, amp VIN. No boost: the GH-S37D servos are rated 3.6-4.8 V, so they
  run straight off the 1S cell (4.2 V full). Nothing feeds the XIAO's 5V pin.
- Battery - -> ground splice -> XIAO BAT-, 4 servo browns, both caps, amp GND, divider bottom.
- Divider middle -> D2 (reads half the battery voltage: 4.2 V full -> 2.1 V; firmware stops walking below 3.6 V -> 1.8 V).
- XIAO 3V3 -> VL53L0X VIN. XIAO GND -> VL53L0X GND. VL53L0X GPIO1 and XSHUT: open.
- CHARGING: plug USB-C into the LEFT side (the XIAO's port) with the switch ON. The XIAO charges the cell at
  ~50 mA (safe for 500 mAh); about 10-12 h from flat, so charge overnight. Its charge LED shows status.
- XIAO pins: D0 touch pad | D1 / D3 / D8 / D9 servos FL / FR / RL / RR | D4 SDA, D5 SCL (distance sensor)
  D6 amp BCLK | D7 amp LRC | D10 amp DIN | D2 battery sense.
- Firmware: camera XCLK on LEDC timer 0, servos on timers 1-3. XIAO mic on I2S0 (PDM), amp on I2S1.
  D6/D7 double as the boot serial port: expect a short click from the speaker at power-on.
- Camera and mic are wired inside the XIAO Sense. Leave its SD slot EMPTY (it shares D8-D10).
- Amp SD and GAIN: unconnected.
- Self-test firmware: firmware/ponder_selftest (Arduino, board XIAO_ESP32S3, PSRAM OPI, library "VL53L0X" by Pololu).
  Flash it, open Serial Monitor 115200: it tests every part and prints PASS/FAIL, then w = walk, d = distance...
- Checks: python fitcheck.py | node erc_check.js (npm i three@0.128.0) | python ponder_sim.py --gif (pip install pybullet)

TRAINING CUBI IN SIMULATION (cubi_train.py, on top of the physics bench)
  pip install pybullet trimesh manifold3d cma gymnasium
  python cubi_train.py gait     CMA-ES gait search, ~4 min on 2 cores. Every generation uses fresh random
                                worlds: desk grip 0.2-0.6, 0-30% heavier, 50-100% servo strength, 70-110% servo
                                speed, 0-40 ms servo lag, +-3 deg desk tilt. Candidates are judged on 12 held-out
                                worlds and only replace the current gait if they beat it.
  python cubi_train.py edge     edge/wall reflex: 8 threshold settings x 6 worlds x 4 approach angles + false alarms
  python cubi_train.py test     trained vs hand-tuned on the same 12 test worlds
  python cubi_train.py export   writes firmware/ponder_selftest/cubi_gait.h (the firmware's walk() uses it)
  python cubi_train.py ppo      optional: a 2x32 neural net that adds +-4 deg corrections on top of the gait
                                (pip install stable-baselines3 torch; a few hours on a laptop). Only flashed if it
                                beats the plain gait by 5% on the test worlds.
  Result (1 Nov): 26.7 mm/s average, 20.6 worst-case on the test worlds vs 16.9 / 13.5 for the hand-tuned gait
  (+58%), for ~20% more servo effort. 12 deg swing, 2.2 steps/s, 35% fast swing, legs trimmed +1.6 / -1.6 deg.
  The step rate is capped at 2.2/s: with no cap it found 3/s (32 mm/s), which leans on the sim's ideal servos.
  Edge reflex: 0 falls in 192 approaches, worst stop 40 mm from the edge, no false stops at any ratio 1.15-1.5
  (a real desk edge drops past the sensor range, so all thresholds trip together; 1.35 kept for small drops).
  Before trusting a trained gait: time one real servo swinging 60 deg on the battery and put it in
  ponder_sim.SERVO['speed']; then compare a real 3 s walk with the sim (expect ~8 cm).

EYES
- Tall ovals: 9 x 12 mm openings in 16.5 x 20 mm bezel rings, centred 1 mm below the camera lens / sensor axis so
  the rings clear the top edge. The distance sensor's 25 deg beam leaves through the lower half of the right eye
  (ray test: worst ray at 0.87 of the oval edge, nothing clipped); the camera sees out to 36 deg off-axis unblocked.
- Camera fit (left eye): the module pushes into an 8.6 mm square socket (8.5 module + 0.05 per side) with 4.6 mm
  deep walls, and the lens barrel sits in a 0.8 mm collar at the back of the oval with 0.2 mm all round. Field of
  view is clear to 40 deg off-axis. PRINT stl/ponder_fit_coupon_camera.stl FIRST (~10 min, same orientation as the
  shell): if the module won't go in, set CAM_SQ = 8.7; if it rattles, 8.55. Lens barrel too tight in the collar:
  measure it and set CAM_LENS_D. A dot of gel CA on one socket wall holds it either way.
- The sensor's view cone is cut only behind the wall, so the face shows a clean oval. For the best look, black out
  the inside of both eyes (Posca / Sharpie / matte acrylic) so the sensor wedge and camera socket vanish.

LIE DOWN (keys L / U; puppy mode lies down when she falls asleep and gets up when you tap her)
- Front legs fold 75 deg forward, rear legs 75 deg back, until she rests flat on the bottom plate (4.9 mm
  thick, so it hangs 2.5 mm lower than a plain 2.4 mm plate). Then the servos switch off: no current while she rests.
- The GH-S37D only travels 90 deg, so the legs go on the splines OFFSET: front legs ~30 deg forward, rear legs
  ~30 deg back, with the servo centred. Walking then uses servo -42..-18 deg (front) / +18..+42 (rear), folding uses
  the far end (45). Firmware: legs[].horn_deg = +30 / -30, and trim_us fixes whatever the spline teeth gave you.
- Why the thick plate: with a 2.4 mm plate the body only touches at ~78 deg of fold (sim), beyond the servo's
  reach. The extra 2.5 mm makes it touch at ~74. Ground clearance while walking drops from 18.4 to 15.9 mm, and the
  plate weighs ~5 g more (sim: walking speed unchanged, 27 mm/s). The screw holes are counterbored 2.5 mm from
  below, so the M2x6 screws still clamp through 2.4 mm and bite ~3.6 mm into the bosses.
- Sim (10 worlds incl. glass, rubber mat, 30% heavier, half-strength servos, +-2.5 deg desk tilt): lies flat on the
  plate every time, servo load ~0 while lying, gets back up every time. On a tilted slippery desk she can slide
  ~2 cm while lying down.

STRETCH (key 8; puppy mode does it every time she wakes up)
- A real puppy stretch, using the folding reach: play bow (front legs out to 72 deg, nose drops ~22 deg, bum bobs),
  then each back leg stretched 60 deg out behind, one at a time. 4.5 s.
- Sim: 0 falls, ~7 mm drift, ~5 deg turn. The bum bob is in phase on purpose: wagging the rear legs in opposite
  phase twists her around ~11 deg.

SHUTDOWN / GOODNIGHT (key Z; puppy mode does it on its own when the battery hits 3.6 V)
- Sequence: settle -> lie down on the bottom plate -> keep folding the legs up beside her -> servos off. Then flip
  the switch. The switch cuts the battery instantly, so the firmware can't see it coming: always Z first.
- Waking (U, or a tap/clap in puppy mode): legs back down to the lying pose, stand up, stretch.
- How far the legs fold is set by the servo, not the shell. Fit check: folding front legs forward and rear legs back
  is clear of the body and the other legs all the way to 180 deg (pointing straight up).
  With the GH-S37D at its rated 90 deg, the legs stop at 75 deg, the same as lying down, so the tuck adds nothing.
  Vertical needs about 210 deg of servo travel (horn offset = travel/2 - 15 = 90).
- Measure YOUR servo before mounting legs: key r, step with < and >, note the pulse at each end, measure the angle.
  Put it in SERVO_TRAVEL_DEG / SERVO_MIN_US / SERVO_MAX_US; TUCK_DEG follows. If you get more travel, also raise
  legs[].horn_deg and HORN_OFFSET_DEG (ponder_parts.py) to travel/2 - 15, capped at 90, and re-mount the legs.
- Viewer: "Shutdown, your servos (90 deg)" and "Shutdown, 180 deg preview" show both cases.

GAIT SMOOTHNESS (sim, 12 test worlds; jolt = RMS body acceleration, rock = RMS roll + pitch rate)
                                speed       jolt        rock       bob
  trained walk (default)        27.0 mm/s   1.17 m/s2   13.2 deg/s  0.56 mm
  same, reversals rounded       30.4        1.16        15.1        0.61     faster, NOT smoother
  smoothest at >= 22 mm/s       21.8        0.94        14.6        0.47
  smoothest at >= 18 mm/s       18.6        0.82        12.0        0.38     'calm' gait (cubi_brain.json gait_calm)
  old hand-tuned                17.1        0.86        10.1        0.36
- The jolt IS the walk: she moves by stick-slip (feet slip forward fast, grip on the slow push back). Searches
  that weighted smoothness either stayed fast and jerky or stopped walking (0.9 mm/s); there's no free lunch.
  Smoother costs speed roughly 1:1.
- Soft start / stop (in firmware walk() and mind-mode walks): stride eases in over 0.5 s and, at the end, she keeps
  stepping while the stride shrinks to nothing over 0.6 s. Start rocking 40 -> 24 deg/s, stop 38 -> 27.
  An edge or wall still stops her instantly.
- CUBI_SMOOTH in cubi_gait.h rounds off each leg's reversals (0..1). Left at 0: it made her faster, not smoother.
- What the sim can't show: servo gear backlash and deadband. Watch the real robot before chasing more smoothness;
  then softer shoes (TPU pads) or a flatter rocker (SHOE_ROCK_R 24-30 mm) are the next things to try.

MIND MODE (key M; firmware/ponder_selftest/cubi_mind.h, tested in cubi_mind.py)
- She does what she wants. No commands: drives build up and she acts on them.
  Traits (rolled on first boot, saved in flash; I = show them, W = roll a new Cubi): bold, social, playful, lazy.
  Drives: energy, curiosity, loneliness, boredom (+ excitement from taps). Each scores a behaviour: explore,
  call you (turns, whines, wiggles, waits 4 s for a tap), play a trick, nap (lies down, servos off, wakes on a
  tap/clap or when rested), look around, sit. She picks with a little randomness: consistent, never a loop.
- GOLDEN RETRIEVER (key G; keeps what she's learned, W goes back to a random Cubi): traits bold 0.6, social 0.95,
  playful 0.85, lazy 0.35, plus habits: praise counts 70% more and fades half as fast, a clap is always a party
  (never startles), after a minute without contact the next touch is a reunion (wag-bounce-wag + yip), she wags
  while sitting, and calls you with a happy yip-bounce instead of a whine.
  Sim (4 days each, same desks): golden 33% exploring, 23% playing, 13% looking for you, 0 falls, 6 reunions,
  0 startles. She praises up faster per tap but plays less than the clown, so a trick takes her a little longer to
  become a favourite in practice.
- Learning: a head tap within 4 s of a trick is praise; that trick gets likelier, ignored ones fade. Saved.
- Reflexes still win: taps / claps / hands interrupt anything, the edge reflex guards every step, glance and bow.
- Sim (41 six-minute days on a finite 50 x 36 cm desk, random worlds, a simulated person coming and going):
  0 falls. Time budget by personality - explorer 51% exploring; cuddler calls for you 14% and naps 20%;
  clown plays 34%. Someone who only praises 'wiggle': it goes from 59-91% of her play on day 1 to 80-94% on day 2.
- Exploring, she glances 25 deg left and right every 2 s of walking. The distance sensor only looks AHEAD:
  walking within ~20 deg of parallel to an edge, her side legs went over before the beam saw it (3 falls in 16
  days before the glance, 0 in 24 after). Cost: exploring is ~1/3 walking, 2/3 glancing.
- Desk memory (dead-reckoned map of edges / visited spots) is in the sim but OFF: without a gyro her heading drifts
  ~1 deg/s and the map didn't reduce edge hits (3.4 vs 3.0 per minute of exploring). An IMU would fix that.

PUPPY MODE (firmware/ponder_selftest/cubi_play.h, tested in cubi_play.py)
- Serial Monitor: P = puppy mode, x = stop, 1-9 = one trick (1 wiggle, 2 paw pat, 3 bounce, 4 chase, 5 zoomies,
  6 sniff, 7 startle, 8 stretch, 9 settle).
- Senses: head tap (copper pad) -> wiggle + trill. Clap -> startle (calm) or bounce (excited). Hand in front of the
  eyes -> paw pat; pull it away -> she chases it. Ignored 5 min -> sighs and sleeps; a tap wakes her with a stretch.
- Mood: excitement jumps on taps (+0.5), claps (+0.3), hands (+0.25) and fades over ~20 s; energy drains while
  playing. High excitement = bounces, wiggles, zoomies (max once a minute); low = sniffing, exploring, settling.
  Never the same trick twice in a row, never the same speed twice (85-115%), 0.5-3 s between ideas.
- Sounds are synthesized on the chip (yip, double yip, whine, sigh, trill, ruff). Previews: sim_out/sounds/*.wav
  (python cubi_play.py sounds).
- Physics checks (python cubi_play.py tricks / soak): every trick stays within +-12 deg of hip swing and under
  4 deg of body tilt on its own (bounce was 17 deg at 8 deg / 5 Hz and resonates at 6 Hz: now 6 deg / 4 Hz).
  Soak: 16 runs x 60 s of random taps, claps and hands with a desk edge 12-30 cm away at up to 50 deg: 0 falls,
  0 tip-overs, 12 edge stops (the reflex beats every forward trick, including zoomies), worst tilt 15 deg.
- The edge sensor only looks forward. Backing up (startle, edge back-off) is blind: startle is limited to once
  every 10 s (~1 cm). Don't put her down with her back to an edge.

HOW IT HAS TO WALK (full physics sim, ponder_sim.py: real leg/shoe meshes, real mass layout, GH-S37D limits)
- Stick-slip: diagonal pairs (FL+RR, FR+RL) alternate; each leg swings forward fast and pushes back slowly.
  The firmware uses the TRAINED gait (cubi_gait.h, above). The hand-tuned bench gait (+-12 deg, 1.5 steps/s,
  20% fast swing) does 24 mm/s on wood, glass-slick (0.2), 30% heavier or half-strength servos; 20 mm/s on a
  grippy mat. Tilt stays under 1 deg.
- Turning: pivot (one side still) ~9 deg/s, or diagonal spin ~9 deg/s. Plain sine waves don't move it.
- Slopes: 5 deg up or down, ~22 mm/s (uphill it drifts sideways: steer it).
- Cables: a loose cable gets shoved along. A TAPED-DOWN cable stops it (hip-only legs can't lift a foot).
- Standing still: stop sending servo pulses; the legs hold straight down.
- Edge + wall reflex (run it in firmware at 50 Hz, never via the agent): at startup read the ToF on flat
  desk (~105 mm). Reading > 1.35x that, or no return = EDGE: stop, walk backward 1.5 s, stop. Reading <
  0.75x = obstacle: stop. Sim: stops 89 mm short square-on, 62 mm at 30 deg, 34 mm at 60 deg blind-side
  first, 85 mm going downhill, also on a slick desk. With the sensor looking straight ahead it walks off.
- Sim note (1 Nov): pybullet multiplies the two surfaces' friction. Earlier runs set it on both the desk and
  the shoes, so every "mu" was really mu squared (more slippery than labelled). Fixed; numbers above are new.

LIMITS
- Hips within +-12 deg (front and rear legs touch at 17 deg).
- Battery (500 mAh): ~1 h 15 min of walking with the camera on (sim: servos 0.13 A avg + XIAO 0.15 A + sensor),
  ~1.8 h idle with Wi-Fi + camera on. Sleep the camera between photos to stretch it.
  Charge AND program over the left-side USB-C (switch ON to charge).
- Vision is snapshots: the XIAO sends a JPEG to the OpenClaw agent on request or every few seconds; a
  vision model describes it (1-3 s round trip). The distance sensor handles edges and walls locally.
- Servos peak ~0.3-0.5 A together (sim); with a loud word + Wi-Fi burst the cell sees ~1.2 A for milliseconds (2C, fine).
- Servo pulses 1000-2000 us only (90 deg servos). Torque drops ~25% as the cell runs down; the sim walks at half torque.
- Cap the speaker volume at ~70% in firmware.
- About 77 g. 48 x 50 x 48 mm body, 64 mm wide over the legs, 69 mm tall, 18 mm ground clearance.
